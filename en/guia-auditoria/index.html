Basic website security audit: what gets checked and why

You do not need to be technical to understand what a basic review looks at. Here is the map, with a checklist to follow.

Important: I only work on sites you own or have written permission from the owner to review. Looking at someone else's systems without permission is not an audit: it is a problem.

1. HTTPS and certificate

The site should always open over HTTPS, with a valid, unexpired certificate issued for your domain. Also check that http redirects to https and that there is no mixed content (images or scripts loaded over http). Want an alert before it expires? See the automation guide.

2. Security headers

HTTP headers tell the browser how to protect your visitors. You can look at them on your own site with:

curl -sI https://yoursite.com

Look for these, among others:

  • Strict-Transport-Security (forces HTTPS).
  • Content-Security-Policy (limits which resources the page can load).
  • X-Content-Type-Options and X-Frame-Options (or frame-ancestors inside the CSP).
  • Referrer-Policy and Permissions-Policy.

A missing header is not a catastrophe, but it is a concrete chance to improve.

3. Access and MFA

  • Unique, long passwords kept in a password manager.
  • Two-step verification (MFA) on hosting, domain, email and the site admin panel.
  • Accounts of people who no longer work with you: removed.
  • Everyone with the minimum permission they need.

4. Updates

Outdated software (CMS, plugins, themes, libraries, server) is one of the most common ways in. Check what you run, which version, and whether there is a process to update. Remove what you no longer use.

5. Backups

An audit also asks whether copies exist, whether they are off the server, and whether anyone has tested a restore. See the backup guide for details.

6. Email: SPF, DKIM and DMARC

These are DNS records that help your email arrive and make it harder for anyone to spoof your domain. SPF says which servers may send on your behalf, DKIM signs the messages and DMARC says what to do when the other two fail. Step by step in the email guide.

Checklist

  • The site opens over HTTPS and the certificate is valid.
  • http redirects to https.
  • I checked the security headers with curl -sI.
  • MFA is on for hosting, domain, email and admin panel.
  • No old users or extra permissions remain.
  • Software and plugins are up to date.
  • Backups exist off the server and I tested a restore.
  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC are set up.
