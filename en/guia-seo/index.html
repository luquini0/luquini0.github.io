Technical SEO: the basics so your site can be found

Technical SEO will not rank you by magic: it removes obstacles so search engines and people can understand and use your site.

An honest note: SEO takes time and results are not guaranteed. Nobody can promise you a specific position on Google; what you can do is get the basics right and improve a bit at a time.

1. Speed and Core Web Vitals

Google measures loading experience with Core Web Vitals (main content load, responsiveness to interaction and visual stability). The basics: compressed, right-sized images, few heavy libraries, decent hosting and caching. You can measure your site with PageSpeed Insights or Lighthouse in Chrome.

2. Indexing and sitemap

  • A sitemap.xml with your important pages, referenced in robots.txt.
  • Verify your site in Google Search Console and submit the sitemap.
  • Check that no important page has noindex by mistake or is blocked in robots.txt.
  • Every page with its own canonical tag.

3. Heading structure

One page, one clear h1 saying what it is about; then h2 and h3 ordered like a table of contents. Give every page its own title and meta description, written for people.

4. Internal links

Link between your own pages with descriptive text ("backup guide" beats "click here"). It helps search engines discover content and people keep reading. Check for broken links too.

5. Structured data

Markup (usually JSON-LD using the schema.org vocabulary) that describes the content: organization, article, product, FAQ. It can help search engines understand your page, but it does not guarantee any rich result. Validate it with Google's public rich results tools.

6. Useful content

No technique replaces answering what your customer is actually searching for. Write to solve a real question, from your experience, in plain language. Update what changes.

Checklist

  • I measured speed and fixed the heaviest things (images first).
  • I have a consistent sitemap.xml and robots.txt.
  • My site is verified in Search Console.
  • Each page has title, description, canonical and a single h1.
  • Pages link to each other with descriptive text.
  • I tested structured data with a validation tool.
  • My content answers real questions from my customers.
  • I understand that results take time.
